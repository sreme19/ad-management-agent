The face-forward, "Tinder-level" version of this hook. Governed by
`rules/creative-generation.md` (POV §1, palette §5, safe areas §7) and
`rules/compliance.md` (§6). Reference: Tinder's Move On Salon — a woman
*narrating her own breakup*, not posed. That is the format this borrows.

**Why this is POV-correct with a visible face:** §1 bans a woman as the *object*
of the frame. Here she is the *speaker* — her story, her eyes, her decision. The
camera is her confidant, not an admirer. If any cut frames her as posed-for-the-
viewer (slow pans up the body, over-the-shoulder glance to camera), that is an §1
`escalate`, not a keep.

## Format

- 9:16 vertical, 1080×1920, Snap Story / Meta Reels. ~15–18s (testimonial needs
  more room than the 6s still; the emotional turn still lands by second 3).
- Produced UGC style — handheld, natural, unpolished-on-purpose. NOT a glossy
  studio spot; the FOURTEEN-SUITORS learning is that cream + ordinary beats
  dark + luxe in this feed.

## Casting

- One Indian woman, reads 25–30. Warm, real, relatable — deliberately NOT a
  glamour/fashion model. She is believable as someone recounting a breakup to a
  friend. Bangalore-appropriate look and accent.
- No man on screen at any point (keeps clear of §6.1 entirely).
- Unbranded contemporary wardrobe, muted. Ordinary room or café, warm daylight,
  cream/neutral palette — never nightclub or dark.

## Script — first person, Hinglish, her own voice

The English pivots are the load-bearing words; the rest code-switches naturally.
Keep it conversational, not read-off-a-card.

| t | Line (spoken) | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | "Breakup ke baad, sab bole — bas move on karo, start swiping again." | Straight to camera, dry, a little tired. The hook lands here. |
| 3–7s | "Par wahi swipe pile, wahi type ke log… nahi. **Move on karna tha — par dhang se.**" | The turn. Slight shrug. This is the idea's headline, spoken. |
| 7–12s | "Yahan har koi verified hai. He's vetted before he ever reaches me — main scroll nahi karti." | Product, in her words. Cutaway allowed here (see shot list). |
| 12–16s | "**Verified, not vibes.**" + end card | Said plain, half-smile. Cut to end card. |

**On-screen type (overlaid, Gabarito, per §2):** the two bold lines above —
"Move on karna tha — par dhang se." and "Verified, not vibes." — burned in as
captions so it reads with sound off. Pink `#FF3B6B` on "dhang se" and "Verified".
**CTA:** `More` (LPV objective, `/get/w`). Not `Sign up`.

## Shot list

1. **A-roll:** her, face to camera, handheld, chest-up, warm window light. The
   whole script can run on this one setup — simplest, cheapest, most honest.
2. **Cutaway (optional, 7–10s):** her hand setting the phone face-down — reuse the
   Variant A plate concept from `prompts.md` as B-roll. If the phone screen shows
   a profile, it MUST be the product's own AI-enhanced, labelled render, never a
   real man's photo (§6.1/§6.2).
3. **End card:** cream ground `#FFF3F0`, "Verified, not vibes." + lowercase
   `riteangle` wordmark. Keep top 10% / bottom 15% clear of the platform chrome.

## The blocker this introduces — app owner's call

A real actor and a shoot cost money. This is the open question
`q-2026-08-26-creator-cost-in-india`: the ~₹50k/month envelope already funds only
one or two ad sets at `budget.md`'s floor, so a creator/actor fee may not sit
beside a funded ad set. Two ways this ships:
- **Produced spot with a hired actor** (one-off fee, no ongoing creator deal) —
  the affordable reading of the standing `ugc-format-womens-creative` idea.
- **A real creator partnership** — stronger, but only if the fee clears the floor
  alongside spend. Unresolved until that question is answered.

Either way it exceeds in-session generation and needs the owner to fund production.
