## What the numbers say

**The monochrome gain is real and it is exactly cancelled by the palette loss.**
t1-bw is the better photograph — black-and-white hides the small AI tells, deepens
the contrast, and genuinely reads more editorial, which is +1.0 on stop-scroll and
+0.5 on craft. It then gives back 4.0 on palette differentiation, because it
abandons the one thing `creative-style.md` says is our differentiator before a word
is read. Net −3.5. The rubric was written before this question came up and it
priced it anyway.

**t2-cream ties the shipped hero, which is the actually useful finding.** The
tighter crop (y=300 rather than y=150) buys stop-scroll and gives back type-safe
space, and it nets to zero. A grade and a crop cannot fix this image.

**Because the real gap is not colour.** Every Bumble frame in
`note-2026-08-27-bumble-tinder-india-13-posts` has someone caught mid-motion: arms
thrown wide laughing on a summit, mid-conversation at a fitness event, six women
packed into frame laughing. Our hero is a woman holding still and looking pleasant.
That is the difference, and no amount of grading produces it. It needs a generation
round against `brief.md`, which is written and ready to dispatch.

## Recommendation

Do not ship either treatment as an improvement — t2-cream is a lateral move and
t1-bw is a net loss on our own rubric. Ship the tighter crop if anything, and spend
the effort on dispatching the round once a browser is reachable.

The yellow question is separate and belongs to the page, not the plate: #FFC629 is
Bumble's own brand colour, and wearing a direct competitor's signature on our
landing page is a positioning decision for the app owner, not a creative tweak. No
candidate here bakes it in.
