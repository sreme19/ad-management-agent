## Idea

A breakup re-entry hook built as Concept B — "Move on, properly." Screen text
"Move on toh karna hai — par dhang se." (you do want to move on, but properly),
tagline "Verified, not vibes." It borrows Tinder's own "move on" language and
turns it: not a donut and a party, a real next person. Visual is her, composed —
not crying, not partying — the deliberate middle Tinder's breakup campaigns skip.

## Rationale

Tinder India's biggest and highest-engagement content bet is the breakup, not the
match (Move On Salon at 7,346 likes is a filmed Mumbai pop-up, plus the Dunkin
Move On Box and DumpYourExCess). Every Riteangle emotional thread is an
entry-moment hook; the exit moment is untested. The Riteangle turn is the opposite
register from Tinder's — dignified continuation, not swipe-pile re-acquisition —
which is exactly what makes it defensible for a matchmaking product rather than a
copy of a swipe app's re-engagement loop. Hinglish carries the hook, per the
same-day learning that the sharpest line in three separate competitors was
bilingual while every Riteangle tagline is English-only.
