## Question

Should Riteangle test Hinglish ad copy against its English-only taglines, and on which audience first?

## Why it matters

Every tagline in rules/creative-style.md is English-only and the repo has never examined ad copy language, yet the two strongest Shaadi.com hooks we have observed are both Hinglish puns, and the comment thread under the Hinge reel shows an English-speaking creator whose audience answers her in Roman-script Hindi. campaigns/women-1822-casual-lpv runs PAN targeting, so the variable is already live and untested. Under rules/creative-generation.md section 8's one-variable-at-a-time rule, a Hinglish/English pair on an otherwise identical plate is a clean test — this question decides whether that test is worth a slot and which audience it runs on.

## Answer (2026-08-29)

Yes, and it is no longer a question of whether a slot is worth spending — the app owner settled it as a direction on 2026-08-28: the MOVE-ON lead funnel ships Hinglish, not English, on the reasoning that the women reachable first are more comfortable in Hindi than English. So the test is running whether or not this question authorised it.

What this pass adds is the part the question left open — WHICH Hinglish. Script is an audience signal, not a styling choice: romanised Hindi is what urban Gen Z actually writes on Instagram and WhatsApp, and an ad set mostly in Devanagari reads as addressed to the Hindi heartland or to an older audience (lrn-2026-08-29-roman-script-is-an-audience-signal). That corroborates from the audience side what lrn-2026-08-27-hinglish-is-shaadis-hook-mechanic found from the creative side: all three sharpest competitor lines it recorded are romanised. So the answer to 'on which audience first' is women 18-30 pan-India in ROMAN script, and Devanagari is a separate later test rather than a free stylistic variation.

TWO THINGS THIS DOES NOT ANSWER, deliberately. First, the clean one-variable Hinglish-vs-English pair that idea-2026-08-27-hinglish-vs-english-headline proposes is NOT what is now running — the lead funnel changes capture point, objective, destination and language at once, so it cannot attribute a result to language. That idea still stands as the only way to actually learn whether Hinglish beats English. Second, nobody has validated that the drafted lines read correctly to a native speaker in the target register, which is a spend gate on the campaign and is tracked separately.
