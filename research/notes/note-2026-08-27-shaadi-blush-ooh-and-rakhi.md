# Shaadi.com — two Instagram posts observed 27 Aug 2026

Both brought in by Sree as screenshots during a co-pilot ad-scanning session. Screenshots saved to
`creatives/_competitor-reference/shaadi.com/`. This is the first competitor asset actually persisted
to that folder — the 26 Aug sweep (`note-2026-08-26-competitor-instagram-sweep`) recorded that it
could not save any, so those posts exist only as prose. These two exist as files.

## Post A — "Blush chahiye?" OOH billboard, co-branded with MARS Cosmetics

File: `creatives/_competitor-reference/shaadi.com/blush-chahiye-mars-ooh.png`
Posted: ~1 week before 27 Aug 2026 (IG showed "1 w"), collab post credited to `shaadi.com` AND
`reachedmars` (MARS Cosmetics, an Indian colour-cosmetics brand). Both handles verified.
Engagement as displayed: **20,200 likes, 313 comments, 131 shares.**

What the image actually shows: a photograph of a real roadside billboard, shot from across a
multi-lane road with motion-blurred traffic in the foreground — a black hatchback, two white sedans,
a yellow-and-black auto-rickshaw, a white Range Rover. Glass-and-concrete office towers and palm
trees behind. Daytime, hazy white sky. Reads as Gurugram/Cyber City or similar NCR business
district. The billboard is split into two red bands, darker maroon above, brighter red below.

Copy, verbatim, top band:
    Blush chahiye?
with the MARS logo set right.
Bottom band:
    Ya blush karane wala?
then a small white pill-shaped button reading `Find your perfect match!` and the shaadi.com logo
(lowercase wordmark, heart over the second "a").

IG caption: "For a match worth blushing over 💕"

The mechanic is a bilingual pun on one English loanword. "Blush chahiye?" = "want blush [the
cosmetic]?" — MARS's product. "Ya blush karane wala?" = "or someone to make you blush?" —
shaadi.com's product. One noun, two readings, split across two advertisers, resolved by the brand
lockup underneath each half. Hindi grammar carrying an English retail noun; no translation offered
and none needed for the target reader.

## Post B — Raksha Bandhan festival creative

File: `creatives/_competitor-reference/shaadi.com/raksha-bandhan-dulhan-ka-bhai.png`
Posted: 3 hours before capture, i.e. morning of 27 Aug 2026. Solo shaadi.com post, not a collab.
Engagement as displayed: **31 likes, 8 comments.** Share count not shown. Story ring on the avatar.

What the image shows: a flat-lay on deep red textured card. A heavy silver curb-link chain bracelet
runs diagonally corner to corner, with an oval turquoise cabochon set in the centre link — a rakhi
styled as men's jewellery rather than the usual thread. Scattered loose grains of white rice and a
small mound of red kumkum powder below it (both standard Raksha Bandhan puja items). shaadi.com
wordmark top right.

Overlay copy, verbatim:
    Happy Raksha Bandhan to
    har dulhan ka BHAI
("...to every bride's BROTHER", with BHAI bolded.)

IG caption, verbatim as far as shown: "Raksha Bandhan? It's done, bro 🥰" followed by "... more"
(caption was truncated in the screenshot; the remainder was not captured).

The angle: Raksha Bandhan is the brother–sister festival, and the brother's traditional vow is to
protect his sister. Shaadi.com maps that vow onto getting her married — the brother as the family
member who takes responsibility for a sister's match. It ties a matrimony product to a festival that
has nothing to do with romance, by routing through the family decision-maker rather than the bride.

## The contrast, which is the reason both are in one note

Same account, same week, same follower base (312K per the 26 Aug sweep). The co-branded OOH photo
took 20.2K likes; the in-house festival creative took 31 in three hours. Three hours is not one week
and the comparison is not clean — but 31 likes at 312K followers is roughly 0.01%, and a post that
was going to travel would not be that flat this far in. The 26 Aug sweep's verdict on this account
was "strong hook, weak engagement." Post B is consistent with that. Post A is not: 20.2K on 312K is
~6.5%, which is an order of magnitude above what that sweep found typical for the account.

Two confounds worth stating rather than hiding: Post A is a **collab post**, so it is served to
MARS's follower graph as well as shaadi.com's, and the like count is pooled across both audiences —
the sweep's per-account denominator does not apply to it cleanly. And an OOH photograph is a
real-world-proof format that reliably outperforms studio creative on social regardless of copy. So
the 650x gap is not purely a copy-quality result. What can be said is narrower: the account is
capable of engagement far above its own baseline, and the two posts that bracket that range differ
in partner, format, and language register all at once.

Not recorded here as a claim about what causes what — that belongs in a learning, and this note is
only what was on the screen.
