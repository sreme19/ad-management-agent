## Setting

(Blue Yeti or similar) standing in shot, bottom left. Orange-red patterned shirt. Karaoke-style
word-by-word captions burned in, keywords highlighted in green and yellow. This is a
podcast/creator-marketer setup — deliberately produced, but produced in the *creator* register, not
the brand register.

## The mechanic, read off the engagement shape

**101 comments against 142 likes.** A comment-to-like ratio of 0.71. For comparison, in the same
session's material: @anerithakkar_ ran 122 comments on 5,499 likes (0.02), @malikkaadvani 21 on
3,229 (0.007), @wingwoman.sim 26 on 1,657 (0.016).

That is a two-orders-of-magnitude difference in shape, and it is not a discussion thread. The caption
asks for a keyword and the comments are people typing it. This is a comment-triggered DM funnel:
the keyword fires an automated reply carrying the link, and the commenter becomes a contactable lead
in her inbox. The post is not competing for reach — it is a lead-capture form wearing a reel.

The second CTA ("grab the link from the front of my page") is the same funnel with the link-in-bio
fallback for anyone who won't comment.

## What she is selling, in her own words

"The 101 most attractive men in my database: serious stable Indian guys ready for commitment."

Three things stacked into one line: a curated finite set (101, a countable shortlist), an
attractiveness ranking, and a commitment-intent filter. The video is the setup for it — the pitch is
that Indian men are passive, egotistical, uncompromising and unhurried, and her database is the
pre-filtered exception to that.

## Bearing on Riteangle

**On the female complaint.** This is the first India-specific articulation in the session's set, and
it is a *supply-quality* argument again, not a volume one: the problem is that the available men are
passive and overestimate their options, not that there are too many of them. Note the direct
collision with the male side — @anerithakkar_'s reel drew "power dynamic" backlash from men, and
here a matchmaker tells the same market that men "think they have more options and better options
than they actually do." Both are describing the same disputed question of who actually holds
leverage.

**On lead capture — and this is the operationally sharp one.** `lrn-2026-08-26-no-lead-capture-on-destinations`
records that Riteangle's paid destinations capture no contactable lead where mass-market Indian
competitors do. This is the same finding one layer earlier in the funnel: the capture is happening
in the *comment section of an organic post*, before any ad spend or landing page exists. Cost per
lead here is zero.

**On what cannot be copied.** `rules/compliance.md` #7 forbids ranking people, and #1 forbids
provider/status framing. "The 101 most attractive men in my database" is a ranking of people by
desirability stated as the product. Riteangle cannot run this line, and the sanctioned phrasing
under #4 — "identity-verified and established professionals" — is deliberately narrower than
"attractive". Her "serious stable... ready for commitment" is closer to sanctioned ground: intent
and stability rather than looks or status. That half is instructive; the ranking half is a contrast
case, not a model.
