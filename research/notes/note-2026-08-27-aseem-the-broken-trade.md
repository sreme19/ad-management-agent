## What is actually being claimed

The argument is structural, not behavioural. The 20th-century relationship was a trade: she brought
childcare and housework, and received love, financial security, and relief from the stigma of being
unmarried. Two of her three returns have since been removed — she earns her own money and the stigma
has weakened — while her side of the ledger did not shrink. So the trade is now lopsided and
declining it is the rational move. Every explanation he rejects in the caption (toxic apps, shy men,
women's standards) he classes as a symptom of that, not a cause.

Note where he puts dating apps: **not the cause, and not even the main symptom.** "Infinite options
of dating apps" appears in a closing throwaway list alongside cost and isolation, after the argument
is already made.

## Limits that must travel with this note

1. **He says "at least in the West," and that qualifier does most of the work.** The two forces the
   argument turns on — women's independent earnings and collapsed stigma around being unmarried — are
   the two that transfer least well to India, where marriage-related social pressure on women is
   materially higher. This is a Western-market argument, quoted here because the *shape* of it is
   useful, not because its premises are established for Riteangle's market. Nothing here should be
   treated as an India finding without separate evidence.
2. **Every statistic is unsourced and secondhand.** "One in three single women", "halved since the
   70s", "four times more than their partners" reach us through a creator summarising a video essay
   he himself misremembered as a documentary. They are recorded because they were said, not because
   they are true. Do not put any of them in copy.
3. **n=1 creator, and he is not the customer.** A man explaining women's incentives is a reading of
   the audience, not the audience speaking.
