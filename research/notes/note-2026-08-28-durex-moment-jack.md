# @durex.india — the one brand-voice account observed that beats every creator, and by 20×

Three screenshots handed over 28 Aug 2026, archived at
`creatives/_competitor-reference/_adjacent-category/durex.india/`. Durex is not a dating app, but it
is the closest adjacent case available: a sexual-wellness brand advertising in India, inside the same
platform policy surface that `lrn-2026-08-26-casual-apps-absent-from-meta` shows swallowing four
Western casual/affair-adjacent brands whole. It is worth recording precisely because it is the
counter-example to a learning this repo already holds.

## Why this matters more than the numbers

`lrn-2026-08-26-brand-voice-organic-is-dead` says brand-voice organic earns nothing in this category
and only creator/UGC or one produced brand film breaks the pattern. Its best counter-case so far was
Aisle's brand film at 6,238 likes and Bumble × @ahillyeah at 11,600. **Durex's Halloween still — a
brand-voice, no-creator, single-image post — took 116,000 likes.** That is roughly 10× the best
creator post ever recorded in this repo and 20× the best produced brand film.

The mechanic is not budget and it is not production. All three are cheap: two are a product pack shot
against a flat background with type on it, and the third is a ball on a bedsheet. What they share is
that **each one hangs on an external moment the audience is already thinking about** — Halloween, a
Spider-Man title, a cricket final — and resolves it with a double meaning the brand never states
outright. The product is present but is never the subject. "No ghosts here, just spirits being
lifted" is a Halloween joke first and a condom ad second, and the viewer has to do the last step
themselves.

**Sliderman shares more than it is liked: 65.8K shares against 37.6K likes, a ratio of 1.75.** No
other post seen anywhere in this repo's competitor set comes close. A film-title parody is a thing you
send to a specific person; a brand statement is not.

## Where this cannot be copied

`rules/compliance.md` §1 forbids money and provider framing, §6.3 fixes 18+, and Riteangle's whole
positioning is anti-casual. Durex's innuendo register is available to a contraception brand and is not
available to a dating app selling seriousness — Meta's dating vertical carries its own written
permission requirement (`lrn-2026-08-28-meta-dating-needs-written-permission`) and an ad that reads as
sexual content in that vertical is the fastest route to losing it. **The transferable part is the
structure, not the tone**: attach to a moment the audience already has in their head, let the product
sit in the corner, and make the last inference theirs. The Riteangle version of "Save some domination
for the bedroom" is not a rude line — it is a cricket-final line about matches, or a wedding-season
line about who is asking, that never mentions swiping.

The #Final post also carries the platform **"AI content"** label, the second such instance in this
capture session after @heynnnow. See `lrn-2026-08-28-india-labelling-duty-is-the-platforms`.

## Limits

Three screenshots, one session. No follower count for @durex.india was captured, so 116K is a raw
count and not a rate. Whether any of these had paid amplification is not knowable from the surface,
and a 116K post on a global FMCG account is exactly the kind of thing that usually does. The date on
the Halloween post is 31 Oct 2025 — nearly ten months before capture — so it is not evidence about
what is working now, only about what worked once. No comment threads were read.
