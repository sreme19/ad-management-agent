# Tinder Move On Salon — corrections and one new co-brand

A grid screenshot of tinder_india brought in 27 Aug 2026, after the original Bumble/Tinder
screenshots were lost. Archived at `creatives/_competitor-reference/tinder_india/tinder-move-on-salon-grid.png`,
plus the Urooj episode's own cover at `tinder-move-on-salon-urooj.jpg` (fetched from shortcode
`DUqQM3oiC4N` on @uroojashfaq's account, visible in the screenshot's status bar).

## Correction to note-2026-08-27-bumble-tinder-india-13-posts

That note recorded Move On Salon as having **two** episodes, ft. Urooj and ft. Prish, because those
were the two thumbnails available. **There are at least three.** The grid shows a third tile,
**ft. Akriti** — Akriti Negi. A Tinder India press release dated 11 Feb 2026 ("Cut Ties, Cut Hair:
Tinder's Move On Salon Delivers Breakup Glow-Ups This Valentine's Day") describes the format as a
**street-side makeover pop-up in Mumbai** hosted by hairstylist Sapna Bhavnani, in which Akriti Negi,
Urooj Ashfaq and Prish traded real breakup stories for a haircut. So the campaign is an IRL
activation filmed for social, not a studio series — which strengthens rather than changes
`lrn-2026-08-27-tinder-sells-the-breakup`.

Engagement confirmed unchanged: the Urooj episode still shows **7,346 likes, 70 comments**.

## Hinglish in the Move On Salon subtitles — a fourth instance

The grid's leftmost tile shows three subtitled frames from an episode, burned in over the footage:

> "I love you" se zyaada mai "sorry" bolta tha
> So that's when you broke up?
> Arey itni jaldi seekhne walo mein se nahi hoon! Chalaya ek aur saal.

("He said 'sorry' more than 'I love you'." / "So that's when you broke up?" / "Hey, I'm not one of
those quick learners! I ran it another year.")

The interviewer's line is English, the participants' are Hinglish, and the subtitles do not translate
— they transliterate. This is a fourth independent Hinglish instance after Shaadi's two puns and
Tinder's metro notice, and the first where Hinglish is used for *testimony* rather than for a
headline gag. See `lrn-2026-08-27-hinglish-is-shaadis-hook-mechanic`, which is already marked
`supported` and is now clearly misnamed after Shaadi.

## New: Tinder x Nykaa, and the second "blush" billboard

One tile is a creative captioned **"Swipe to see who we are flirting with >>>"**, built as a jigsaw
puzzle over a photograph of a real roadside billboard reading **"Blush?"** with the **NYKAA**
wordmark beneath it. Nykaa is India's largest beauty retailer. Tinder is running an OOH
flirtation with a cosmetics brand, and revealing the partner by having the audience "swipe" the
puzzle apart.

**This is the second dating-brand blush billboard with a cosmetics partner observed in one day.**
`note-2026-08-27-shaadi-blush-ooh-and-rakhi` records Shaadi.com x MARS Cosmetics running
"Blush chahiye? / Ya blush karane wala?" on a Gurugram-area billboard roughly a week before capture.
Same product category, same word, same medium, two rival dating brands. Whether one is answering the
other or both are working the same obvious pun independently cannot be told from what is here — but
the Shaadi execution resolves the pun in Hinglish and Tinder's is English-only with the reveal held
back for the swipe.
