## Claim

Google Flow gates spending behind a free text-only storyboard stage and an explicit credit-cost approval, and carries a first-class reusable character that can be recalled by @name across prompts

## Evidence

- (2026-08-28) (2026-08-28) Kevin Stratvert Flow walkthrough, no affiliate or product being sold. Flow does not generate on the first prompt: it asks follow-up questions, returns a WRITTEN storyboard (characters with ages, locations, props, wardrobe, and per-frame context/action/camera/lighting/dialogue), and states plainly that editing at that stage costs nothing because only video generation consumes credits. Generation is then gated behind a confirmation naming the price - six clips, 90 credits, an Approve button. Separately, the characters panel saves a named character from a text description OR an uploaded reference image, assigns it a voice, and recalls it in any later prompt by typing @name; the demo generated a character, then placed the same face in an unrelated setting. Two consequences here: the unrecorded cost and reference-status of the round-3 candidate (q-2026-08-28-storyboard-grid-vertical) were operator omissions, not tool limitations; and bake-off idea C, cast one Indian woman once and reuse her, is a built-in feature rather than a workaround. Not evidence about output quality or performance - this is a mechanics walkthrough, n=1 demo, on a cookie commercial.
