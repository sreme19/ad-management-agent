## Claim

Shaadi.com's strongest-performing recent creative works through a bilingual pun that only resolves if you read Hindi and English together, and no Riteangle tagline is written that way

## Evidence

- (2026-08-27) The 'Blush chahiye? / Ya blush karane wala?' OOH billboard took 20.2K likes and 313 comments, against ~0.01% engagement on the same account's in-house Raksha Bandhan post three hours old the same week. The pun turns on one English retail noun read twice through Hindi grammar. The 26 Aug sweep already recorded Shaadi's other standout hook, 'Sirf scroll karoge ya kabhi phere bhi loge?', as Hinglish too — two for two. Every tagline in rules/creative-style.md is English-only, and no learning, question or idea in the repo addresses ad copy language at all, while campaigns/women-1822-casual-lpv runs PAN targeting.
- (2026-08-27) **supported**: Third independent instance, and the first from a brand other than Shaadi. Tinder India's Baddies Coach metro campaign runs a parody public-safety notice in Hinglish: 'Kripya peeli rekha se door khade rahein. Aur red flags se bhi. Issued in Baddie Interest by TINDER' — please stand away from the yellow line, and from red flags too. The pun needs both languages at once and it parodies the 'Issued in Public Interest' format of a real Delhi Metro notice. With Shaadi's 'Blush chahiye? / Ya blush karane wala?' and 'Sirf scroll karoge ya kabhi phere bhi loge?', three of the four sharpest lines observed across every brand in two sessions are bilingual puns. The claim should probably be renamed off Shaadi. See note-2026-08-27-bumble-tinder-india-13-posts.
