## Claim

Flow's saved-character reference is unreliable across generations and degrades as the number of characters in a scene rises, so cast consistency cannot be assumed from having built characters

## Evidence

- (2026-08-29) Observed across four clips generated 2026-08-29 for the MOVE-ON lead funnel, all prompted with explicit @name references to characters saved in Flow's Characters panel plus a 'do not invent a new character' instruction. Single-character scenes held the face but drifted on detail: Meher's hairband appeared in one panel of a three-panel strip and not the other two, and Kavya's library clip returned a sleeveless ribbed tank rather than the linen kurta her saved character specifies — a garment near-identical to what Anaya wore in the adjacent office scene, which would have read as one woman in two rooms. The four-character wedding scene was the clear failure: the faces do not match any of the four saved characters, a fifth woman appears despite an explicit 'no fifth person' instruction, and the wardrobe returned as sarees rather than the specified kurtas. CONSEQUENCE FOR PLANNING: a narrative that depends on the same face appearing in two scenes — here, the drained woman in scene 1 being the laughing woman in scene 4 — cannot be relied on and should not be the load-bearing idea of a cut. Group scenes are where it breaks worst, which argues for three figures rather than four and for per-clip editing over regeneration when a detail is wrong. Bears on q-2026-08-28-storyboard-grid-vertical's second half, which asked whether round 3's character was referenced or invented: this session says reference adherence is weak enough that the question is the wrong shape — it is not binary per generation.
