## Claim

Tinder India's biggest content bet is the breakup rather than the match, and it is their highest-engagement material

## Evidence

- (2026-08-27) Four distinct Tinder India campaigns observed are all about ending a relationship: Move On Salon with Sapna Bhavnani (post-breakup haircuts, 7,346 likes on the ft. Urooj episode — the highest single number across every brand post captured in this session), the Tinder x Dunkin Move On Box counter-programmed against Valentine's week 7-14 Feb, #DumpYourExCess 2.0 which pays a month of Tinder Plus for blocking your ex, and Axe Your Ex. They are buying attention at the moment of exit from someone else's relationship rather than at the moment of wanting a new one. Riteangle's rules/creative-style.md emotional threads are all entry-moment: the desolate man, the flooded woman, the camera as judge. Caveat: engagement is not conversion, and a breakup hook suits a swipe app's re-acquisition loop in ways it may not suit a matchmaking product.
- (2026-08-27) **supported**: Move On Salon is confirmed as at least a three-episode series (ft. Akriti Negi, Urooj Ashfaq, Prish), and Tinder India's own 11 Feb 2026 press release describes it as a street-side makeover pop-up in Mumbai where participants traded real breakup stories for a haircut. So the highest-engagement asset in the observed set (7,346 likes) is not a studio production but a filmed IRL activation — the breakup bet is bigger and more operationally committed than a content series.
