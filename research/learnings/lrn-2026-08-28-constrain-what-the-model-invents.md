## Claim

Every workable AI-creative route converges on the same move: constrain what the model invents, and never let it invent human performance

## Evidence

- (2026-08-28) Four independent sources reached the same structural conclusion in the 28 Aug 2026 pass. (1) riteangle-photo-engine-eval.pdf, 29 Jun 2026: editing a real photo held identity where recreating the person drifted - see lrn-2026-08-28-edit-framing-beats-recreate. (2) The Google Flow tutorial's three-rung ladder: text-to-video 'is the one you control the least... it invents the whole scene and all of the motion from scratch'; frames hands the model an approved still so 'it's not inventing the scene anymore. It's only filling in the motion'; ingredients locks multiple approved references so that 'every one of them is locked in before the video even starts'. (3) Kling Motion Control, demonstrated in the second tutorial: 'copy the movement from one video and apply it to another character' - a real person performs and the model only reskins, so timing, weight and micro-expression are human while the face is generated. (4) The LexHive ads team's own resolution of the same problem by hiring human creators for final assets once AI has tested the concept (lrn-2026-08-28-ai-tests-humans-ship). Where the four differ is only in what supplies the human constraint: a real photo, an approved still, a real performance, or a real creator. Caveats: both tutorials are commercially motivated (one runs a Higgsfield affiliate link, the other sells a course), neither is evidence about ad performance, and Motion Control specifically is unverified on price, 9:16 support, Indian faces, and whether Higgsfield is the only route to it.
