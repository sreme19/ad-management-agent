## Claim

Romanised Hindi is the script for urban Gen Z social copy and Devanagari signals a Hindi-heartland or older audience, so script choice is an audience signal rather than a styling preference

## Evidence

- (2026-08-29) An academic source on advertising in India's Hindi belt (Signs and Society, University of Chicago Press) states the advertising implication directly: an ad written almost entirely in Devanagari signals to the viewer that it is targeting a completely different audience, typically Hindi heartland regions rather than urban youth. Roman script dominates among youth on Instagram, WhatsApp and Twitter; Devanagari appears in formal posts, news and older demographics. Mechanical reasons given: Devanagari typing friction on phones, no keyboard switching, and easier code-mixing with English. This corroborates from the audience side what lrn-2026-08-27-hinglish-is-shaadis-hook-mechanic found from the creative side — all three of the sharpest competitor lines it recorded (Shaadi's 'Blush chahiye? Ya blush karane wala?' and 'Sirf scroll karoge ya kabhi phere bhi loge?', Tinder's 'Aur red flags se bhi') are romanised, not Devanagari. Bears directly on the MOVE-ON lead funnel, whose copy the app owner asked to be Hinglish with 'some Hindi words'.
