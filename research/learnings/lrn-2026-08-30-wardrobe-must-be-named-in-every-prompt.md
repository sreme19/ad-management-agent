## Claim

Generation engines inherit clothing from reference frames and will not correct implausible wardrobe for the activity, so kit must be named explicitly in every frame prompt

## Evidence

- (2026-08-30) Two Act 3 beats in the Riteangle cut returned wardrobe that undercut their own argument: the competitiveness beat showed her serving on a tennis court in jeans and a loose t-shirt, and the career beat showed her presenting to a seated boardroom in a grey vest top. Both were inherited from the source frames rather than invented at the animation step, and neither prompt had named the clothing. CONSEQUENCE FOR PLANNING: on an ad whose argument is competence and self-development, wardrobe is load-bearing rather than decorative. A woman serving in jeans reads as a casual knockabout, not as someone focused on her game.
